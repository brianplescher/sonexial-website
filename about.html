The Methodology

Why signal architecture, not marketing strategy.

Most author marketing advice treats discoverability as a promotion problem — better ads, more reviews, a bigger platform. Those things matter downstream. But they work on top of a substrate that either supports them or defeats them: the metadata layer Amazon actually uses to classify and surface your book.

My academic background is in information systems and structured learning environments — how information gets organized so that the right people find it. Amazon's ranking mechanism is an information retrieval problem. BISAC codes are a classification system. Keyword strings are query-matching infrastructure. When I looked at the author discoverability problem this way, I stopped asking "how do I market my book better?" and started asking "what signals is Amazon actually reading, and are mine accurate?"

The gap between a well-written book and a well-discoverable book is almost never about the writing. It's about whether the signal layer underneath is correctly assembled.

The kits Sonexial offers are the operationalization of that analysis — built from research into how Amazon's browse and search systems work, applied to real titles with real results, and stress-tested on my own publishing work before being offered to anyone else. The human review layer exists because algorithmic outputs require editorial judgment to be useful. That judgment comes from working the problem as an author, not just as a researcher.
