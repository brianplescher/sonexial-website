## The Sonexial Method

### Amazon Algorithm Principles
- Amazon categorizes books by metadata signals, not by reading them
- The description must contain the keywords the algorithm expects for the genre
- HTML formatting (bold, bullets) increases conversion — Amazon renders it
- Long-tail keywords outperform broad terms because competition is lower
- Category selection is as important as keywords — wrong category = invisible
- The "Also Boughts" are driven by accurate categorization, not just ads

### What Breaks Discoverability
- Generic descriptions that don't signal genre clearly
- Keyword stuffing that reads as spam
- Wrong BISAC categories
- Author bio that doesn't establish genre credibility
- Descriptions written for humans, not for the algorithm + human combination

### What Works
- Opening line that names the genre and hooks the reader simultaneously
- Middle section that hits genre tropes and reader expectations
- Closing line that creates urgency or FOMO
- Keywords embedded naturally, not forced
- HTML bold on the most important phrases

## Output Standards

### Descriptions
- Always HTML formatted (use `<b>` for bold, `<br>` for line breaks)
- Opening hook: 1–2 sentences max
- Body: 3–4 short paragraphs hitting genre beats
- Closing: urgency or call to action
- Total: 150–200 words optimal

### Keywords
- Always long-tail (3–5 words minimum)
- Include search intent in the phrase where possible
- No single generic words
- Formatted as a numbered list with a one-line rationale for each

### Bios
- Third person
- Genre credibility first, personal details second
- 75–100 words for standard, 150–200 for authority-building variant
